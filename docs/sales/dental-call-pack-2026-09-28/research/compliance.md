# Compliance research — Sydney dental cold-calling, 28 Sep 2026

Prepared for M&U Ventures (Usman + Mehroz), owner-led B2B cold calls to Sydney dental
practices on Monday 28 September 2026, plus at most one follow-up email per practice
where the practice asked for it. This is our operational reading of publicly available
official guidance, not legal advice. All sources are official Australian regulators/
legislation.gov.au family sites, fetched 27 September 2026. Where something could not be
verified from an official source it is flagged **UNVERIFIED** and we default to the more
conservative house rule.

---

## Rules for Monday — read this before you dial (≤60 seconds)

1. **Only call business-published numbers of the practice** (website/Google listing). Business
   landlines and mobiles are not eligible for Do Not Call Register listing (fax-only business
   numbers are the one exception), so the Register itself does not block these calls. —
   [Do Not Call Register FAQs](https://www.donotcall.gov.au/consumers/faqs-for-consumers), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
2. **Call only Mon–Fri, 9:00am–8:00pm, or Sat 9:00am–5:00pm.** No Sunday calls, no calls
   before/after those windows. — [Telecommunications (Telemarketing and Research Calls)
   Industry Standard 2017, ss 8(1)–8(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L00323/asmade/2017-03-28/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
3. **No calls at all on the listed national public holidays** (New Year's Day, Australia Day,
   Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, Christmas Day, Boxing Day, and their substitute
   days). — same Standard, s 8(3), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
4. **House rule (not required by the Standard's wording): treat all times as Sydney/NSW
   local time**, since the Standard's clock is pinned to the "usual residential address of the
   account-holder" — a concept built for consumers, not businesses. Using Sydney time keeps
   us compliant regardless of how a business "account-holder" is read. UNVERIFIED how the
   Standard applies its residential-address time rule to a business account-holder — see §2.
5. **Open every call with: your real first name, "M&U Ventures", who you're calling on
   behalf of (yourselves), and the purpose of the call**, before pitching anything. —
   Standard, s 9(2), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
6. **Never block or withhold your caller ID.** Calling line ID must be on and able to receive
   a return call for at least 30 days. — Standard, s 14, accessed 27 Sep 2026.
7. **If the person asks you to stop or end the call, end it immediately** — no "just one more
   thing." — Standard, s 13(1), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
8. **Do not record your own sales calls.** NSW law requires consent (express or implied) from
   everyone on a private conversation before it's recorded; we have no consent mechanism on
   a cold call. — [Surveillance Devices Act 2007 (NSW), s 7](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2007-064), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
9. **Only send a follow-up email if the practice actually asked for it on the call** ("send me
   something") — that's our express consent under the Spam Act; don't email practices who
   didn't ask. — [Spam Act 2003, s 16 & Sch 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A01214/latest), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
10. **Every follow-up email must clearly identify M&U Ventures as sender and include a
    working unsubscribe link**, honoured within 5 business days if ever used. — Spam Act 2003
    (Sch 2) via [ACMA – Avoid sending spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
11. **Don't offer to publish patient testimonials or before/after photos** on any site we
    pitch, and don't promise clinical/patient outcomes in the pitch itself — that's the
    practice's regulatory risk under s 133, and ours by association if we suggest it. —
    [Health Practitioner Regulation National Law (NSW), s 133](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2009-86a) and
    [Dental Board of Australia advertising guidelines](https://www.dentalboard.gov.au/Codes-Guidelines/Advertising-a-regulated-health-service/Guidelines-for-advertising-regulated-health-services.aspx), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
12. **Don't say "never miss a call" or promise guaranteed revenue/ROI from the AI
    receptionist.** Describe what it does; don't promise outcomes we can't substantiate. —
    [ACCC – False or misleading claims](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
13. **Only collect business contact details** (practice name, reception/owner contact,
    business email/phone) during the call — no patient or personal data about staff beyond
    what's needed to follow up.
14. **When pitching the AI receptionist, tell prospects it discloses to their patients that
    they're speaking with an AI system and that calls may be recorded** — there's no binding
    Australian rule requiring this for phone AI yet, but it's our house standard and ACMA has
    just started requiring AI disclosure in the adjacent radio-broadcast code, signalling the
    direction of travel. — [ACMA – AI disclosure required under new commercial radio
    rules](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-02/ai-disclosure-required-under-new-commercial-radio-rules), accessed 27 Sep 2026 (UNVERIFIED for phone calls specifically — see §8).
15. **House rule: cap it at one follow-up email per practice, full stop** — even with
    consent, don't turn "send me something" into a drip sequence.

## 1. Do Not Call Register Act 2006

**The law says:**
- Business telephone numbers are **not eligible for registration** on the Do Not Call
  Register, except business numbers used primarily for fax. A number used for both personal
  and business purposes can be registered only if it's used mostly for private/domestic
  purposes. — [Do Not Call Register – FAQs for consumers](https://www.donotcall.gov.au/consumers/faqs-for-consumers), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- Practical effect: a dental practice's main published business landline/mobile cannot be on
  the Register, so calling it does not breach the Act's core prohibition (calling a
  registered number without consent). We could not find an official source stating in so
  many words "the Act does not apply to B2B calls" — the FAQ content available to us
  addressed eligibility for registration, not a general B2B carve-out from the Act itself.
  **UNVERIFIED**: whether the Act's other provisions (e.g. exempt organisations, telemarketing
  definitions) contain any separate B2B trigger we haven't sighted; we did not find one.
- Consent under the Act can be **express or inferred**; consent **cannot be inferred merely
  from publication of a phone number**. Express consent that isn't given for a specific or
  indefinite period lapses after **three months**. Inferred consent generally requires an
  existing, relevant commercial relationship (e.g. an existing customer of the same
  organisation) — not something we have with a prospect we've never spoken to. —
  [Do Not Call Register Act 2006 (Cth), Sch 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2006A00088/2021-09-01/2021-09-01/text/original/pdf), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Washing**: telemarketers calling numbers must check ("wash") their calling list against
  the Register, with lists no more than 30 days old at time of calling, via a paid
  subscription account. — [Do Not Call Register – Using the register](https://www.donotcall.gov.au/industry/industry-overview/using-the-register/), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- A **free washing tier exists** for ad hoc telemarketing/fax marketing of up to 500 numbers
  per year (Subscription Type A, no fee) — found via search of donotcall.gov.au content, not
  independently confirmed on the fetched "Using the register" page (which described the paid
  subscription requirement generally but not the fee-free tier in the content we could
  retrieve). **Flagged as needing re-verification** before relying on it operationally,
  though it is consistent with the register's published industry-overview material.

**Practical rule for Monday:** because we are calling published, non-fax business numbers,
not numbers drawn from a purchased consumer list, the Register's core prohibition doesn't
bite and washing is not required for this campaign. If M&U later builds a larger outbound
list that could include personal/dual-use numbers, wash it first.

## 2. Telecommunications (Telemarketing and Research Calls) Industry Standard 2017

**The law says** (from the Standard's own text):

- **Permitted calling times for telemarketing calls** (non-research):
  - Weekday: not before 9am, not after 8pm
  - Saturday: not before 9am, not after 5pm
  - Sunday: **no calls**
  - Specified national public holidays: **no calls** all day (New Year's Day, Australia
    Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, Christmas Day, Boxing Day, plus any
    substitute public holiday)
  — [Telecommunications (Telemarketing and Research Calls) Industry Standard 2017, s 8(1)–(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L00323/asmade/2017-03-28/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Time zone basis**: the Standard defines the relevant time as the time at "the usual
  residential address of the relevant account-holder" (s 8(4)). This wording is written for
  a residential consumer, not a business account-holder — **UNVERIFIED** how it applies
  cleanly to a business number with no "residential address." Our house rule: treat every
  call as bound by Sydney/NSW local time regardless, so we're covered under any reading.
- **Required disclosures at call start** (non-research calls): the caller's given name, the
  company/registered business name on whose behalf the call is made, and the purpose of the
  call — s 9(2).
- **Calling line identification**: must be enabled, not blocked, and capable of receiving a
  return call for at least 30 days — s 14.
- **Ending the call**: the caller must immediately terminate the call if the recipient asks
  for it to end or otherwise indicates they don't want it to continue — s 13(1).
- **Application to B2B / non-Register numbers**: the Standard applies to "participants in
  ... the telemarketing industry" making calls to "an Australian number" generally (ss 5–6).
  We found **no explicit carve-out** in the sections retrieved that exempts calls to business
  numbers or numbers not on the Do Not Call Register — meaning the Standard's calling-hours,
  disclosure, caller-ID and termination rules appear to bind our calls **even though the
  practice's number itself can't be on the Register**. Treat the Standard as fully binding.

**Practical rule for Monday:** call only Mon–Fri 9am–8pm or Sat 9am–5pm Sydney time, open
every call with your name + "M&U Ventures" + purpose, keep caller ID on, and end the call the
moment anyone asks.

## 3. Spam Act 2003

**The law says:**

- Commercial electronic messages need **consent** — express or inferred. Consent can't be
  inferred purely because an address was published; it generally requires an existing
  relevant relationship or the recipient making the address available for a purpose that
  covers the message. — [Spam Act 2003 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A01214/latest), accessed 27 Sep 2026, corroborated by [ACMA guidance on avoiding sending spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Conspicuous publication exception**: if someone has conspicuously published their business
  electronic address (e.g. on the practice website), a commercial message can be sent to it
  **without separate consent**, provided the message relates to that person's business role
  or function, and there is no notice against unsolicited messages next to the published
  address. Consent still cannot be inferred from mere publication alone in other contexts —
  the conspicuous-publication exception is a distinct, narrower carve-out tied to work
  relevance. — ACMA guidance, accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Sender identification**: every commercial electronic message must clearly identify the
  sending organisation and include accurate contact details.
- **Unsubscribe**: every commercial electronic message must include a functional,
  no-cost unsubscribe facility that works for at least 30 days, and any unsubscribe request
  must be honoured within **5 business days**. — ACMA guidance and Spam Act Sch 2, accessed 27 Sep 2026.

**Practical rule for our single follow-up email:** send it only when a practice explicitly
asked for it on the call ("send me something") — that's express, on-the-record consent, the
strongest basis available and sufficient on its own without needing to rely on the
conspicuous-publication exception. Identify M&U Ventures clearly as sender, and include a
working unsubscribe link even for a one-off email. Send at most one email; don't build a
sequence out of a single "send me something."

## 4. Privacy Act 1988 / Australian Privacy Principles

**The law says:**

- **APP 7 (direct marketing)** restricts organisations from using or disclosing personal
  information for direct marketing outside specific conditions, and requires a free,
  reasonably-timed opt-out. APP 7 does **not** apply to marketing already covered by the Do
  Not Call Register Act or the Spam Act (i.e. those Acts, not APP 7, govern our calls and
  email). — [OAIC – Chapter 7: APP 7 Direct marketing](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-7-app-7-direct-marketing), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Small business exemption**: organisations with annual turnover of $3 million or less are
  generally **not covered by the Privacy Act at all**, unless an exception applies — e.g. they
  are a health service provider, they "trade in" personal information for benefit (buying or
  selling personal-information lists), they're a contracted Commonwealth service provider,
  a residential tenancy database operator, a credit reporting body, an AML/CTF reporting
  entity, or they've voluntarily opted in. — [OAIC – Small business](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
  This blanket small-business exemption **has not yet been repealed**; removal is proposed
  for a future tranche of reform, not yet legislated. — same OAIC page context, accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- Applying this to M&U: M&U Ventures is under the $3m turnover threshold, is **not itself** a
  health service provider (the dental practice is), and is not "trading in" personal
  information in the sense of buying/selling lists — it is collecting basic business contact
  details (name, practice, phone, email) to pursue its own sales, which the OAIC's own
  small-business page frames as the kind of activity the exemption is meant to cover. On this
  reading, **APP 7 most likely doesn't bind M&U's own outbound cold-calling/emailing**. This
  is **our reading, not a determination from OAIC**, and is flagged accordingly — the OAIC
  material is general guidance, not a ruling on M&U's specific facts.
- **Collect only business contact info** during the call regardless of the exemption analysis
  above — it keeps us inside the safest, least contestable interpretation and matches what
  we actually need.
- **APP 8 (cross-border disclosure)**: an APP entity disclosing personal information to an
  overseas recipient generally remains accountable for how that recipient handles it, unless
  an exception applies (e.g. informed consent, or the recipient is bound by a substantially
  similar law). — [OAIC – Chapter 8: APP 8 Cross-border disclosure](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-8-app-8-cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
  This is directly relevant to the **MU-Receptionist product** (not the cold-calling
  campaign itself): it uses US-based Retell and LLM providers, which means caller/patient
  personal information collected by the receptionist is disclosed overseas. If the small
  business exemption doesn't cover the receptionist service specifically (e.g. because a
  dental-practice client is itself a health service provider bound by the Privacy Act, and
  M&U is processing that client's patient data as a contracted service), APP 8 obligations
  could flow through the client relationship even if M&U's own marketing is exempt. This is
  **not our determination to make unilaterally** — flag to clients that patient call data
  goes to US-based processors, and note it in the receptionist contract/terms, separate from
  the cold-calling compliance question this file covers.
- **Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024**: received Royal Assent 10 December
  2024. Most provisions commenced with Assent; the new **statutory tort for serious invasions
  of privacy** commenced **10 June 2025**; new **automated decision-making transparency**
  requirements (APP 1 additions requiring disclosure of ADM use in privacy policies) commence
  **10 December 2026** — not yet in force as at 27 Sep 2026. —
  [Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 (Cth), C2024A00128](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00128/asmade), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
  Relevance to us: the receptionist's call-routing/triage logic may count as automated
  decision-making once that provision commences in December 2026 — note it for a future
  review, not an action item for Monday's calls.

## 5. Recording phone calls — NSW Surveillance Devices Act 2007 vs Commonwealth Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979

**The law says:**

- **NSW Surveillance Devices Act 2007, s 7**: prohibits installing, using or maintaining a
  listening device to record, monitor or listen to a **private conversation** to which the
  user is not a party, and — critically — even a party to the conversation must not record it
  without the **consent (express or implied) of the principal parties**, unless a specific
  exception applies (e.g. reasonably necessary to protect that party's lawful interests). —
  [Surveillance Devices Act 2007 (NSW), s 7](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2007-064), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Commonwealth Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979** separately governs
  interception of communications passing over the telecommunications network without
  authorisation; it operates alongside (not instead of) NSW's device-based consent rule, and
  where they overlap the stricter requirement applies in practice. —
  [Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/Series/C2004A02124), accessed 27 Sep 2026 (series page; specific interception/party-exception clauses not independently re-verified for this note — treat as **UNVERIFIED in detail**, rely on the NSW consent rule as the operative constraint).
- Net effect for a receptionist product: if MU-Receptionist records inbound patient calls,
  the caller must be told at the start of the call that it may be recorded, giving them the
  chance to object — that's how implied consent is established in practice.

**Our house rule:** **our own owner-led sales calls on Monday must not be recorded.** We have
no consent mechanism on a cold call, so recording a prospect without telling them and getting
their agreement would breach NSW law. Don't record. If we ever want call analytics on our own
sales calls, we'd need to say so at the start of the call and get an audible "that's fine" —
out of scope for Monday.

## 6. Dental advertising — Health Practitioner Regulation National Law s 133 and Ahpra/Dental Board guidance

**The law says:**

- **s 133** makes it an offence to advertise a regulated health service, or a business
  providing one, in a way that: is false, misleading or deceptive; uses testimonials; offers
  a gift/discount/inducement without stating its terms; creates an unreasonable expectation
  of beneficial treatment; or directly/indirectly encourages indiscriminate or unnecessary
  use of the service. A National Board's advertising guidelines can be used by a court as
  evidence of what compliant advertising looks like. — [Health Practitioner Regulation
  National Law (NSW), s 133](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2009-86a), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Testimonials**: any statement addressing clinical aspects of care (symptoms, diagnosis,
  treatment outcomes, practitioner skill) counts as a testimonial and is **prohibited** in
  advertising the practice controls — even if the patient is happy to give it, and even if it
  originated as an organic Google review the practice then features/republishes. —
  [Dental Board of Australia — Guidelines for advertising a regulated health service](https://www.dentalboard.gov.au/Codes-Guidelines/Advertising-a-regulated-health-service/Guidelines-for-advertising-regulated-health-services.aspx), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- **Before/after photos**: not banned outright, but tightly conditioned — consistent framing/
  lighting/angle/background, no editing, only the treated area changes, and a clear statement
  that results vary. Getting this wrong is a common enforcement trigger.
- **Unreasonable expectations**: advertising must not claim treatment is infallible,
  guaranteed or miraculous, and must not minimise risk.

**Practical rule for (a) any dental site we build:** don't feature patient testimonials or
review quotes referencing clinical outcomes anywhere on the site, including a curated
"Google reviews" widget that surfaces clinical commentary; if before/after photos are used,
they need matched conditions, consent, no editing and a "results vary" disclaimer, and the
practice — not us — owns that clinical judgement call; don't write guarantee/miracle-style
copy.

**Practical rule for (b) our sales pitch:** don't suggest to a prospect that we'll help them
publish patient testimonials or promise patients specific outcomes — that's their regulatory
exposure, and recommending it makes us look either uninformed or complicit. We can talk about
professional design, functionality, and the AI receptionist's operational role without
touching s 133 territory.

## 7. ACCC / Australian Consumer Law — claims about AI capability and savings

**The law says:**

- Claims must be truthful, accurate and based on reasonable grounds; a business must be able
  to substantiate any claim it advertises. Predictions or claims about future matters
  (including revenue/ROI outcomes) need reasonable grounds that existed **at the time the
  claim was made** — a claim made without that basis can itself be misleading even if it
  later turns out true. Material limitations can't be hidden in fine print. —
  [ACCC — False or misleading claims](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- "AI-washing" (overstating what the AI does, or its role) is flagged by the ACCC as a live
  enforcement focus, with the maximum corporate penalty now the greater of $100 million,
  3× the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted turnover for the relevant period. —
  [ACCC / Treasury — Review of AI and the Australian Consumer Law, Final Report, October 2025](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/p2025-702329-fr.pdf), accessed 27 Sep 2026.

**Practical rule for Monday:** don't say "never miss a call" (an absolute reliability claim we
can't back), don't promise a specific dollar figure or percentage revenue uplift, and don't
claim the AI "understands" or "thinks" beyond what it actually does. Describe features and
what we've seen in practice, without guaranteeing outcomes.

## 8. Disclosing that a caller is speaking with an AI (voice agents)

**What we found:**

- We found **no binding Australian law or regulator rule specifically requiring outbound or
  inbound phone AI voice agents to disclose "you are speaking with an AI"** as at 27 Sep 2026.
  This is genuinely **UNVERIFIED / not established** — we searched ACMA, and found nothing
  phone-specific.
- The closest official signal: ACMA's **Commercial Radio Code of Practice** changes require
  disclosure when a **synthetic voice** hosts a regularly scheduled radio program or news
  broadcast — a broadcasting-specific rule, not a telephony rule, and not directly applicable
  to a dental practice's AI receptionist. — [ACMA — AI disclosure required under new
  commercial radio rules](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-02/ai-disclosure-required-under-new-commercial-radio-rules), accessed 27 Sep 2026.
- International context (EU AI Act transparency obligations from August 2026, various US
  state rules) is moving toward mandatory AI disclosure, but none of that is binding in
  Australia and it is outside this research's official-source scope — noted for awareness
  only, not treated as an Australian rule here.

**Our house rule (voluntary, not legally required today):** MU-Receptionist should open
patient-facing calls with a brief disclosure that the caller is speaking with an AI system
and that the call may be recorded, and give a clear path to reach a human. We hold ourselves
to this regardless of the absence of a binding Australian requirement, both because it's
good practice and because the regulatory direction (radio code, EU rules) suggests phone-
specific requirements may follow. This is a product-design recommendation for
MU-Receptionist, not itself a rule for Monday's sales calls (the owners aren't using an AI
voice agent to make the cold calls).
