THE BRAND

Deep blue, a lantern and a glass molar.

Lantern Dental is a fictional practice in Rozelle, Sydney. The current flagship direction uses a calm deep-blue palette and opens on a scroll-driven film: as the visitor scrolls, a lantern slowly lights a glass molar while the promise holds still over it — you'll know the plan, the fee and the time before you start. An interactive visit sequence then explains the patient journey before anyone books. The film is AI-generated and labelled as such on the site. It is calm, clinical and distinct from M&U Ventures' own black and gold, Aldergate's property editorial system and Marden & Rowe's courtroom-timber legal identity.

Sixteen pages carry it: a home page, a treatments overview with six individual treatment pages, a page introducing the team, new-patient information, fees and payment, FAQs, and contact and location — the same up-to-eight-page shape we'd scope for a real practice, extended here to show the full booking system working.

JOURNEYS DEMONSTRATED

A six-step booking flow, checked against a live diary.

The booking flow is not a form that emails a request into the void. It reads real availability from a Postgres-backed diary and writes a real row when a patient submits it.

1. New or returning patient
2. Appointment type
3. Practitioner preference
4. Live availability, read from the diary
5. Minimal contact details
6. Review and submit
Booking: appointment type and practitioner preference, checked against live availability

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

Some bookings confirm instantly. Others wait for the clinic.

A returning patient booking a check-up gets an instant confirmation — the slot is theirs. A new patient or an urgent visit becomes a request sitting in the clinic's queue, awaiting confirmation, because those need a human to look at them first.

We tested the constraint that makes this trustworthy: three simultaneous booking attempts for the exact same slot. One won. The other two were refused, cleanly, by a database constraint — not a client-side check that a fast enough double-click could slip past.

FEES AND TREATMENTS

Sample fees in plain figures, and Ahpra-aware treatment pages.

The fees and payment page sets out sample fees plainly, before a patient has to call and ask. Every treatment page is written without outcome claims, before/after imagery or clinical testimonials — the same standard we'd hold a real practice's copy to under Ahpra's advertising guidelines.

The visit sequence: we talk, we look, you decide

CLINIC DASHBOARD

Demo access, no password — the whole day, run from one screen.

The dashboard is what the clinic actually runs from: a day calendar per practitioner, the requests queue with confirm or decline, status changes for completed, no-show and cancelled visits, weekly availability editing and closures, appointment types and durations, a patient contact list, and previews of what a notification would say — nothing is actually emailed or texted from the demo.

The clinic dashboard: day calendar, requests queue and availability editing

WHAT'S REAL, WHAT'S SIMULATED

The booking logic is real. The notifications are not.

Real: the Postgres-backed diary, the database constraint that stops double bookings, and demo-access authentication in front of the clinic dashboard. Simulated: outbound email and SMS — the dashboard shows exactly what a confirmation or reminder would say, and sends nothing.

Real — appointment data persists in Postgres, not local storage
Real — the double-booking guard is enforced by the database, not the browser
Real — the clinic dashboard sits behind demo authentication
Simulated — email and SMS notifications (previewed only, nothing sent)
Simulated — payments and calendar sync
The dashboard's requests queue, confirming or declining a booking on a phone
